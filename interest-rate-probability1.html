How to read the table:
  • Cut / Hold / Hike – the probability that the policy rate after the given meeting will be lower / unchanged / higher than today (cumulative, measured from today's level).
  • Change (bp) – expected change in the rate at this single meeting. 25 bp = 0.25 percentage points.
  • Cumulative (bp) – sum of expected changes from today up to and including this meeting.
  • * – provisional date, the central bank may still change it.
Frequently asked questions
What are central bank rate probabilities?

They are market-implied estimates of how likely a central bank is to cut, hold or hike its policy rate at each upcoming meeting. They are derived from the OIS (Overnight Index Swap) market and updated automatically.

What do cut, hold and hike mean?

Cut means the policy rate is lowered, hold means it stays at the same level, and hike means it is raised. The percentages in the table show the probability of each outcome after the given meeting, measured from today's rate.

What are basis points (bp)?

A basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point. A change of 25 bp equals 0.25 percentage points.

How often is the data updated?

The page refreshes its data every 30 seconds while it is open in a browser. The valuation time and the last update time are shown at the top of the page.

Which central banks are covered?

The Federal Reserve (USD), European Central Bank (EUR), Bank of England (GBP), Bank of Japan (JPY), Bank of Canada (CAD), Reserve Bank of Australia (AUD), Swiss National Bank (CHF) and Reserve Bank of New Zealand (NZD).

What is the VIX?

The VIX, or Cboe Volatility Index, measures the market's expected volatility of the S&P 500 over the next 30 days. It is calculated from S&P 500 option prices and is often called the market's "fear gauge" because it tends to rise during periods of uncertainty.

What do the VIX levels mean?

Below 15 suggests a calm market and a risk-on mood. Readings from 15 to 20 indicate normal or moderate uncertainty. Between 20 and 30 signals heightened uncertainty and risk-off sentiment. From 30 to 40 points to strong market stress, and above 40 indicates extreme volatility and fear.

Which currency has the advantage in risk-off conditions?

The upcoming meetings of central banks should be compared. During risk-off periods, safe-haven currencies such as the Swiss franc (CHF), Japanese yen (JPY) and US dollar (USD) have theoretically tended to strengthen. However, basis-point (bp) values do not always indicate which currency actually has the advantage in risk-off conditions, so this should be taken into account when analyzing the data.

Examples:

  1. If NZD shows +20 bp and CHF shows +4 bp, the rule suggests that CHF should strengthen in risk-off conditions. However, it is better to choose other market conditions listed below.
  2. If NZD shows +10 bp and CHF shows +20 bp, these are ideal risk-off conditions for CHF to strengthen, as the expected move should be stronger and larger.
  3. If NZD has a 70% probability of a rate hike and +20 bp, and CHF also has a 70% probability of a rate hike and +20 bp, the conditions for CHF to strengthen are more favorable than in the first example.
Which currency has the advantage in risk-on or neutral conditions?

In risk-on and neutral conditions, basis-point (bp) values decide which currency has the advantage. The upcoming meetings of central banks should be compared. In risk-on conditions, it is best to choose currency pairs where the base currency is significantly stronger than the quote currency, e.g. NZD (+20) / CHF (+5). This allows us to take advantage of the relative strength of one currency over the other.

How often is the VIX updated?

The VIX value on this page refreshes automatically about once a minute while the page is open. VIX data is delayed by approximately 15 minutes and should not be used as real-time trading data.

Why is the VIX change shown in red or green?

The percentage next to the VIX value shows how much the index has moved since the previous close. A rise is shown in red because a higher VIX usually signals more uncertainty and greater risk aversion, while a fall is shown in green because it usually points to calmer conditions. The colour reflects the direction of the VIX itself, not whether a particular currency or asset is going up or down.

What factors influence rate probabilities and basis-point values?

Rate probabilities and basis-point values move as new information about the economy and central bank policy arrives. The main drivers include:

  1. Central Banks – central bank decisions, interest rates and speeches.
  2. Economic Releases – macroeconomic data compared with forecasts.
  3. Institutions – reports and forecasts from major banks.
  4. Headlines – economic and market news.
  5. Geopolitical Risks – wars, elections, conflicts and tariffs.

Because these factors change often, the bp values shown here can move quickly before a meeting.
